Background (Working on finding the right words :) )

I grew up in a small town in Vermont, where everyone in the state felt like a neighbor. Surrounded by nature, I pursued a passion in horseback riding by sneaking out to clean stalls for a free ride at age ten. I continued to train as a working student in competitive show jumping and finished high school early to pursue it full time. After committing to it seven days a week, for 12+ hour days, I realized that I wanted and needed to do more for myself and for others in my community. I was fortunate to be able to scrape together applications for undergrad, and got into what was later revealed as the same college my French grandmother was one of the first international students at, Connecticut College.

The longer version

The odd jobs that sustained me through those years only reinforced my knack for business, people, and thinking strategically about what's next. Although I believed I would be like one of the scientists my dad's microscope refurbishing business helped, I found economics instead, and the rest was history. My curiosity was activated and I pursued micro to macro mechanisms, studying international relations alongside economics, because how could you only think domestically? I was entranced by the sterile models that made up a world of aggregate behavior, but somehow had the behavioral aspects stripped out of them. I did everything I could to expose myself to behavioral economics: taking a course in Stockholm over the summer, because I didn't have enough credit capacity to take a full semester abroad, and driving to as many talks, conferences, and other events at the Harvard Kennedy School as I could to understand how experts think about these mechanics in the real world.
